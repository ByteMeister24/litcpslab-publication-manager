## Modeling and Verification of Reconfigurable Distributed Control Systems

Authors: Friederike Bruns; Bianca Wiesmayr; Felix Schmid; Lisa Wünderlich; Andreas Rauh

Author Affiliations: * DCIS, Carl von Ossietzky Universität, Ammerländer Heerstraße 114-118, Oldenburg, Germany; LIT, Johannes Kepler University, Linz, Austria; LIT, Johannes Kepler University, Linz, Austria; * DCIS, Carl von Ossietzky Universität, Ammerländer Heerstraße 114-118, Oldenburg, Germany; * DCIS, Carl von Ossietzky Universität, Ammerländer Heerstraße 114-118, Oldenburg, Germany

Keywords: Mixed-Criticality; Network Communication; Flexible Reconfiguration; Contract-Based Design; Modeling; Timing; Contracts; Design methodology; Printing; Production; Real-time systems; Protocols; Decentralized control; Lithium-ion batteries

Abstract: Distributed control systems increasingly rely on heterogeneous, mixed-criticality communication that must meet diverse real-time requirements, adapt to dynamic modes, and ensure safety and correctness. Traditional design workflows often treat communication and control logic separately, limiting early verification and complicating system integration. The proposed design flow supports offline verification of reconfigurable communication architectures while ensuring predictable execution under strict timing constraints. Communication is modeled explicitly through logical messages and physical channels in a contract-based design flow, allowing integration of real-time or quality-of-service properties into the application model. This enables the specification of assumption-guarantee contracts, supports early syntactic and semantic checks, and incorporates dynamic validation of time traces. A battery production case study demonstrates communication heterogeneity and dynamic operating modes in a tool-supported modeling environment.

## Rule-based Redesign of Abstract GraphML Representations into IEC 61499-Oriented Designs

Authors: Shubham Sharma; Oleksandr Kudriavchenko; Felix Schmid; Alois Zoitl

Author Affiliations: CDL VaSiCS; CDL VaSiCS; LIT CPS Lab, Johannes Kepler University Linz, Linz, Austria; CDL VaSiCS

Keywords: GraphML; IEC 61499; Rule-based redesign; Interface strategies; Pre-design analysis; CPPS; IEC; Modeling; Design methodology; Software; Abstracts; Printing; Indium phosphide; Joining processes; Technology

Abstract: This paper presents an approach for deriving IEC 61499-oriented designs from abstract GraphML representations. GraphML is a graph-based language that can represent systems, their components and interactions as a graph. Such representations are useful for communicating top-level system designs and onboarding new team members. However, they mainly capture structural information and lack the IEC 61499-specific design decisions required for control software development. These decisions include the number and type of interfaces, hierarchy levels, and interface connections across nested structures. In systems with many components and deep hierarchies, manually selecting an interface strategy and analyzing its effect on the overall system structure is cumbersome and error-prone. To address this issue, we propose a rule-based redesign approach that redesigns abstract GraphML representations into IEC 61499-oriented GraphML representations. The resulting design preview supports the review and analysis of key IEC 61499 design decisions before implementation. The approach is grounded in practical field experience and discussions with control software engineers, and it supports maintainable and cost-effective control software development.
